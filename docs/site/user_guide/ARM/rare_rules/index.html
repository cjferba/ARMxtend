ARM.rare_rules and FIM.minimal_rare_itemsets -- rare combinations of frequent things

Some of the most interesting patterns are infrequent: two drugs that are each common but rarely given together and then cause an event, a fault that follows a particular pair of alarms. To reach them with a minimum support you have to lower it until nearly every itemset is "frequent", and millions come out. Rare-itemset mining looks only where it matters.

from ARMxtend.ARM import rare_rules
from ARMxtend.FIM import minimal_rare_itemsets

# a and d are frequent (0.30 each) but together only 0.15; b is frequent and goes alone
baskets = [["a", "d"]] * 15 + [["a"]] * 15 + [["d"]] * 15 + [["b"]] * 55

minimal_rare_itemsets(baskets, max_supp=0.2)
#  support itemsets
#     0.15      a,d

rare_rules(baskets, max_supp=0.2, min_conf=0.5)
# antecedents consequents  support  confidence     lift
#           a           d     0.15         0.5 1.666667
#           d           a     0.15         0.5 1.666667

Definitions

An itemset is frequent if its support is at least max_supp and rare if it is below. A rare itemset is minimal (an MRI) if all its proper subsets are frequent: it is rare only because of the combination. The MRIs are a compact basis: any rare itemset is an MRI or contains one. min_supp and min_count are floors against noise (what happens once or twice is not a pattern); an itemset is rare when min_supp <= support < max_supp and it appears in at least min_count transactions.

The MRIs are found level by level, as in Apriori: frequent itemsets of size k-1 are joined; a frequent candidate keeps growing, a rare one is an MRI and stops (its supersets would not be minimal). The result is exact; the tests compare it with the definition applied to every itemset.

Rare rules

rare_rules splits every MRI of two or more items into X => Y. Since all proper subsets of an MRI are frequent, both X and Y are frequent: the rule is rare only through the combination, and its confidence and lift tell whether that combination is more than chance. The output is the usual rule frame (all the measures), sorted by decreasing confidence and support, with attrs n_transactions and n_candidates (the splits examined) so that significant_rules and prune_redundant_rules work as with any other rules.

In the shop example a flat miner with a threshold low enough to see the rare pairs (0.01) returns 47 rules with lift of 2 or more, many of them variations of the same pair (diapers, fruit => wipes); rare_rules returns the 3 that matter.

Signature

minimal_rare_itemsets(transactions, max_supp, min_supp=0.0, min_count=1, min_len=1, max_len=None)
rare_rules(transactions, max_supp, min_supp=0.0, min_count=2, min_conf=0.6, min_lift=None,
           max_antecedent=None, max_consequent=None, max_len=None)

minimal_rare_itemsets returns support / itemsets sorted by decreasing support; attrs['n_frequent'] is the number of frequent itemsets visited (for comparison with ordinary mining).

Things to know

  • max_supp is the knob. It defines what is frequent, so it decides which combinations are minimal; changing it changes the rules, not just how many there are.
  • A rare single item has no rules. It is an MRI of size one and needs two items to be split. Rules between a rare item and the rest are not produced because their antecedent or consequent would not be frequent.
  • Supersets of an MRI are not generated (bread, wine => cheese when wine => cheese is already there). That is the point of a compact basis, but it means that a rare combination that needs a third item to appear is only found if its pairs are frequent.
  • Rare combinations of independent items have lift near 1. In the example, every pair of a common item and a baby product is rare, with lift about 1; min_lift (or a significance test) is what separates them from the real ones.
  • min_count=2 by default in rare_rules (and 1 in minimal_rare_itemsets): a rule seen once has no weight as a rule.

Command line: armxtend rare-rules.

References

  • L. Szathmary, A. Napoli, P. Valtchev (2007). Towards rare itemset mining. Proceedings of the 19th IEEE International Conference on Tools with Artificial Intelligence, 305-312 (minimal rare itemsets and the Apriori-Rare algorithm).
