FIM.taxonomy -- item hierarchies and generalized itemsets

Products come in categories (Jacket -> Outerwear -> Clothes), diagnoses in chapters, sensors in rooms. A taxonomy states those "is-a" relations so that patterns can be found at every level: nobody buys exactly the same jacket and boots, but "outerwear goes with footwear" can be a strong pattern. This page covers the hierarchy and the frequent itemsets over it; the rules are built in ARM.generalized_rules.

from ARMxtend.FIM import Taxonomy, generalized_itemsets

taxonomy = Taxonomy.from_paths(["Clothes/Outerwear/Jacket", "Clothes/Outerwear/Ski Pants", "Clothes/Shirts",
                                "Footwear/Shoes", "Footwear/Hiking Boots"])
baskets = [["Shirts"], ["Jacket", "Hiking Boots"], ["Ski Pants", "Hiking Boots"], ["Shoes"], ["Shoes"], ["Jacket"]]

generalized_itemsets(baskets, taxonomy, min_supp=0.3)
# {Clothes} 0.67   {Footwear} 0.67   {Outerwear} 0.50   {Outerwear, Footwear} 0.33   {Outerwear, Hiking Boots} 0.33 ...

Taxonomy

A directed acyclic graph child -> parent; an item may have several parents. It can be built from

Taxonomy({"Jacket": "Outerwear", "Outerwear": "Clothes"})        # {child: parent} or {child: [parents]}
Taxonomy([("Jacket", "Outerwear"), ("Outerwear", "Clothes")])    # pairs (child, parent)
Taxonomy(frame)                                                  # DataFrame: first column child, second parent
Taxonomy.from_paths(["Clothes/Outerwear/Jacket"], sep="/")       # paths from the root to the leaf

Cycles and self-parents are rejected. Items that only appear as parents are roots, and items that are not in the taxonomy are treated as roots with no descendants, so a taxonomy can cover just part of the catalogue.

Method Meaning
parents(i), children(i) direct relatives
ancestors(i), descendants(i) all of them, without the item itself
is_ancestor(a, i) is a a (strict) ancestor of i?
level(i) 1 for the roots; otherwise 1 + the highest level among its parents, so an ancestor always has a lower level
at_level(i, L) the item seen from level L: itself if level(i) <= L, else its deepest ancestors of level <= L
roots(), leaves(), items, depth, to_frame() the structure

Functions

extend_transactions(transactions, taxonomy)                  # each basket + the ancestors of its items
generalize_transactions(transactions, taxonomy, level)       # each item replaced by its ancestor at `level`
generalized_itemsets(transactions, taxonomy, min_supp, max_len=None)
  • extend_transactions is the base of generalized mining (Srikant and Agrawal, 1995): a basket with Jacket also "contains" Outerwear and Clothes.
  • generalize_transactions is the one-level-at-a-time approach (Han and Fu, 1995): mine the baskets rolled up to level 2, then to level 3, with the usual tools (fpgrowth, association_rules).
  • generalized_itemsets mines all the levels at once on the extended baskets and returns the usual support / itemsets frame (so ARM.association_rules takes it). It never builds an itemset that contains an item and one of its ancestors ({Jacket, Outerwear}): its support equals that of the itemset without the ancestor, so it only adds noise and an exponential number of useless itemsets (the optimization of Srikant and Agrawal's Cumulate algorithm). The candidates are counted with one boolean column per item; memory is proportional to transactions x frequent itemsets of the widest level.

One minimum support per level

Categories are far more frequent than the products below them, so a single threshold either drowns you in category-level itemsets or hides every product-level one. min_supp can be a dictionary level -> support:

generalized_itemsets(baskets, taxonomy, min_supp={1: 0.6, 2: 0.3, 3: 0.1})

A level that is not listed uses the value of the deepest listed level above it ({1: 0.6, 3: 0.1} gives level 2 the value 0.6). An itemset must reach the highest threshold among the levels of its items, i.e. the one of its most general item: {Jacket, Footwear} needs the level-1 support even though Jacket is level 3. This keeps the anti-monotone property (adding an item never lowers the threshold, so every subset of a frequent itemset is frequent) and therefore the search is exact; it is this library's choice and not the only possible one (see the notes in ARM.generalized_rules).

References

  • R. Srikant, R. Agrawal (1995). Mining generalized association rules. Proceedings of the 21st VLDB Conference, 407-419.
  • J. Han, Y. Fu (1995). Discovery of multiple-level association rules from large databases. Proceedings of the 21st VLDB Conference, 420-431.
