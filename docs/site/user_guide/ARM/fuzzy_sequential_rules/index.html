ARM.fuzzy_sequential_rules -- fuzzy "if X happens, Y happens afterwards"

The fuzzy version of ARM.sequential_rules: the events carry a degree (the temperature was low to degree 0.9), and a rule X => Y is valid in a sequence to a degree. Every day, patient or run contributes what it really shows instead of being counted in full or not at all.

from ARMxtend.ARM import fuzzy_sequential_rules

rules = fuzzy_sequential_rules(days, min_supp=0.1, min_conf=0.5, max_antecedent=1, max_consequent=1)
rules[["antecedents", "consequents", "support", "confidence", "lift"]]
# {temp_low} => {power_high}   support=0.22 confidence=0.63 lift=2.76

days has the format of FIM.fuzzy_sequential: lists of events {item: degree}, built by hand or with fuzzy_sequences_from_memberships from a table of memberships.

Definition

With T the t-norm (min or product), the degree of X => Y in a sequence is

mu_X(k) = T over x in X of  max over p <= k of d(p, x)     -- to what degree all of X has appeared by event k
mu_Y(k) = T over y in Y of  max over p >  k of d(p, y)     -- to what degree all of Y appears after event k
degree(X => Y) = max over k of T(mu_X(k), mu_Y(k))

and the measures are the means over the sequences, with the same names as in the crisp case:

  • support(X => Y) = mean degree of the rule;
  • antecedent support (consequent support) = mean over the sequences of T_x max_p d(p, x): to what degree all the items of X (of Y) appear, in any order;
  • confidence = support(X => Y) / antecedent support; lift and the other measures of ARM._measures are computed from the three supports and added as columns, so the table is filtered, sorted and exported like any other rule table.

With degrees 0 and 1 every number equals the one of sequential_rules (checked in the test suite). fuzzy_rule_degree(sequence, antecedent, consequent, tnorm) gives the degree of one rule in one sequence, which is useful to see which days fulfil a rule and how well.

Signature

fuzzy_sequential_rules(sequences, min_supp, min_conf=0.8, max_antecedent=2, max_consequent=2,
                       metric="confidence", min_threshold=0.0, tnorm="min")
fuzzy_rule_degree(sequence, antecedent, consequent, tnorm="min")

The result is sorted by decreasing confidence and support, and result.attrs["n_sequences"] holds the number of sequences. metric / min_threshold add a second filter, as in sequential_rules.

How it is mined

A rule can only reach a support s if the itemset X u Y reaches s when the sequences are seen as baskets (the degree of an itemset in a sequence is the t-norm of the best degrees of its items, which can only go down when items are added). The miner therefore finds the frequent itemsets level by level and tests every split of each one into X and Y within the size limits, evaluating the rule degree of all the sequences at once with cumulative maxima. Memory is proportional to sequences x longest sequence x distinct items.

Reading the numbers

  • A fuzzy confidence is not comparable one-to-one with a crisp one. In the example of examples/example_fuzzy_sequences.py the crisp rule cold => peak has confidence 0.96 with a cut at 12 degrees and 0.82 with a cut at 14, while the fuzzy temp_low => power_high has 0.63: a mildly cold morning is only partly "low" and the evening after it only partly "high", so it adds a small amount to both the numerator and the denominator. What the fuzzy rule does not need is the cut point (the crisp support of the same rule goes from 0.21 to 0.62 as the cut moves from 8 to 14 degrees), and it tells how well each day fulfils the rule (fuzzy_rule_degree).
  • The statistical tests of ARM.significance count transactions and do not apply to fuzzy supports; for fuzzy sequential rules use the supports and lift as a ranking and validate on held-out sequences.
  • As with crisp rules, sort by lift to leave out the rules that are true but uninformative.
  • min_degree in fuzzy_sequences_from_memberships matters: with triangular memberships almost every reading has some degree on its neighbouring labels, and the resulting tiny degrees add noise (and work). 0.1-0.3 is a reasonable range.

Command line: armxtend fuzzy-sequential-rules.

Reference

  • P. Fournier-Viger, C.-W. Wu, V. S. Tseng, L. Cao, R. Nkambou (2015). Mining partially-ordered sequential rules common to multiple sequences. IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering 27(8), 2203-2216 (the crisp rule notion this page fuzzifies).
