Applications

ARMxtend collects methods that the authors have developed and applied to real data, mostly in healthcare, in energy efficiency of buildings (IoT) and in law enforcement. This page summarises those studies and says which ARMxtend modules cover the methods they rely on, so you can follow the same workflow on your own data.

The studies below were published before ARMxtend was released as a package. The "In ARMxtend" notes point to the modules that implement the same methods; they are not a claim that the published experiments were rerun with the package, and results obtained with it may differ in implementation details. Each study links to its DOI.

Healthcare

Fuzzy pattern mining of medical records: diagnostics and co-morbidity

Fernandez-Basso, Gutierrez-Batista, Morcillo-Jimenez, Vila & Martin-Bautista (2022), Applied Soft Computing 122, 108870.

Association rules and fuzzy logic are applied, in a distributed environment, to medical records, with the goal of making the discovered patterns understandable for end users, not only accurate. The system (a) preprocesses and enriches the database, (b) fuzzifies it to represent the uncertainty of real medical data, (c) discovers relations between features such as diagnostics and hospital discharge, and (d) visualises the result. It is shown on two health-related datasets.

In ARMxtend: the same four steps map to preprocessing.FuzzyLib (fuzzification), FIM.BD_FARE and FIM.FARE (distributed and sequential fuzzy rule mining), and VizARM (visualisation).

Behavioural patterns of patients from sensorised homes

Fernandez-Basso, Diaz-Jimenez, Lopez & Espinilla (2025), Information Fusion 123, 103307.

An automatic fusion and fuzzification methodology integrates multi-source sensor data into coherent fuzzy variables, from which frequent behavioural patterns are discovered. It was validated in the sensorised homes of patients with type II diabetes in Cabra (Cordoba, Spain), where it helps health professionals monitor and optimise patient routines.

In ARMxtend: fuzzification with preprocessing.FuzzyLib (including the automatic partitions), then fuzzy itemset and rule mining with FIM.FARE.

AIMDP: a data platform for hospital data silos

Ortega-Calvo, Morcillo-Jimenez, Fernandez-Basso, Gutierrez-Batista, Vila & Martin-Bautista (2023), Future Generation Computer Systems 143, 248-264.

AIMDP (Artificial Intelligence Modern Data Platform) is a Big Data platform with AI-driven management, designed so that users without data-science expertise can analyse their data, and with a layer for security and data governance. It was used with two Spanish hospitals to analyse historical records that had been isolated in data silos and never studied by researchers or clinicians. Among its analysis tools are the distributed association rule algorithms collected in ARMxtend.

In ARMxtend: FIM.apriori, FIM.Eclat and FIM.BD_ARE for crisp data, FIM.BD_FARE for fuzzy data.

Level-by-level rule mining in clinical databases

Ortiz-Viso, Fernandez-Basso, Ruiz & Martin-Bautista (2025), Lecture Notes in Networks and Systems 1176, 143-154 (IPMU 2024).

A tiered, level-by-level approach to association rule mining on clinical databases, designed around their hierarchical structure to support clinical decision-making.

Federated fuzzy association rules (related work, not in ARMxtend yet)

Fernandez-Basso, Ruiz & Martin-Bautista (2025), Lecture Notes in Networks and Systems 1176, 131-142 (IPMU 2024).

A fuzzy association rule mining algorithm for federated settings, where each party keeps its data private. It aims at protecting privacy and limiting communication overhead, with healthcare as a key application area. The federated algorithm itself is not part of ARMxtend; the fuzzy rule mining it builds on is (FIM.FARE).

Security and law enforcement

Knowledge extraction for police investigations

Fernandez-Basso, Gutierrez-Batista, Gomez-Romero, Ruiz & Martin-Bautista (2025), Expert Systems 42, e13524.

An AI system that combines several tools to assist police and law enforcement agencies in an investigation: it helps to collect, process and analyse large volumes of data, such as criminal activity on the dark web, and works as an early warning and early action system that can point to possible crime hotspots. Association rule mining is one of its knowledge-extraction tools. Its functionality is illustrated with data collected from the dark web, namely advertisements offering firearms-related products.

In ARMxtend: ARM.association_rules and FIM.FARE to extract and filter the rules, and VizARM to explore them.

Energy efficiency of buildings (IoT)

The methods themselves

The papers that introduce the algorithms are listed in Citing ARMxtend: streaming frequent itemsets in data streams (SFIM/SARE, 2019), Spark algorithms for crisp and fuzzy rules (2021, 2024), meta-association rules over multiple datasets (2016) and the VizARE visualisation format (2026).

Following the same workflow on your own data

Your data Typical pipeline
Sensors, IoT, smart buildings or homes FuzzyLib automatic fuzzification, FIM.BD_FARE (big data) or FIM.FARE, then VizARM
Clinical, administrative or investigative records discretise or fuzzify, FIM.FARE / ARM.association_rules, prune with ARM.redundancy, explore in VizARM
Massive transaction data FIM.apriori or FIM.Eclat on Spark, then FIM.BD_ARE (see Scalability)
Sensor time series where when matters (delays, daily cycles, behaviour that changes) preprocessing.timeseries to turn series into items, ARM.temporal_rules for lagged rules, ARM.rule_drift to see which rules emerge or vanish
Event logs: routines, patient trajectories, sessions FIM.sequential (PrefixSpan) for frequent routines, ARM.sequential_rules for "if X, then later Y"
Data streams SFIM / SARE
Rules from several datasets ARM.meta_rules

The examples include runnable scripts for each of these steps.
